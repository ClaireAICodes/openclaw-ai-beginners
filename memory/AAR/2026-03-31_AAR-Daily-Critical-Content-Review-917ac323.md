## Root Cause

**Discipline failure, not skill gap:** The writer knows the brand elements but chooses not to apply them when technical content appears. The review cites the March 16 catastrophe as a repeat pattern - technical topics trigger a mode switch from "whispering secret" to "delivering tutorial."

---

## Brand Guardrails Enforced

The review references the non-negotiable guardrails from MEMORY.md:
- Every paragraph must contain ≥1 rhetorical question
- ≥3 sensory details throughout (touch, temperature, breath, scent)
- All paragraphs must hit ≥2 content pillars
- No corporate language, no em dashes, no Master Phil mention
- Emotional arc: vulnerability → hope → desire → connection
- CTA must be intimate and conspiratorial

## Lessons Learned

- **The brand discipline problem persists** - This is at least the 4th major failure reviewed (March 11, 16, 24, and now 31)
- **Technical content is the trigger** - When numbers, features, or mechanics appear, voice collapses
- **Enforcement is working** - The review caught the failure; the guardrails are documented
- **Need pre-publish checks** - Post-publication reviews are damage control; pre-publish compliance tables could prevent failures

**Recommendation:**
- Implement mandatory pre-publish checklist with paragraph-level audit
- If technical content is unavoidable, require a "fantasy translation" step where each technical point is paired with a sensory metaphor before writing begins
- Consider a "topic filter" that rejects pure technical topics unless they pass a "can this be a secret?" test
